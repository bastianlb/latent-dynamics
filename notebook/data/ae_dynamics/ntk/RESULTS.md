# Why do memorized AE train points decouple error from latent typicality? NTK / step-decomposition study

Scripts (in /homes/lbastian/latent-dynamics): `run_ae_ntk_ckpt.py` (re-runs of the dense configs with model+Adam checkpoints at epochs 1..2000, steps s and s+1; final models bit-identical to grid, max |diff| 0.0), `ae_ntk.py` (phase 1, GPU 1), `ae_ntk_selftest.py`, `ae_ntk_analyze.py`, `ae_ntk_plot.py`, `run_ae_lrctl.py` + `ae_ntk_lrctl_report.py` (per-block lr control; `train.py`/`config.py` got `--lr_mult_enc/--lr_mult_dec`, default 1 = unchanged). Data: `ana/` (per-ckpt kernels, 1.8 GB), `metrics.json`, `tables.md`, `figures/fig1..fig7`.
Runs: MNIST AE 784-256-64-d16, Adam 1e-3, bs128, 3 seeds (0,1,2) each of N=250/1000 x wd {0,1e-4}, N=60000 wd0, and linear AE N=250/1000. All numbers seed mean +- sd (n=3; sd is a spread, not an inferential CI).

## Method (what is measured)
Probe: 200 train + 200 test points per run (random fixed subset), at each checkpoint theta_s with Adam preconditioner D=1/(sqrt(v/bc2)+eps) from the s+1 state.
* **G** = loss-gradient kernel <grad L_i, D grad L_j> (EXACT; L_i = per-sample pixel MSE = training loss). It is what tells how a step on j changes L_i. Split into encoder / decoder blocks.
* **T** = pixel-output (784-d) empirical NTK trace kernel Tr(J_i J_j^T), plain D=1, via Hutchinson with K=64 Gaussian output probes (a random 784->64 sketch, unbiased). Selftest vs exact 784-output kernel: diag corr 1.000, median diag rel. err 0.7%, off-diagonal cosine rmse 7e-4 (K=64). The D-preconditioned T was NOT used: it is dominated by zero-gradient parameters with D~1/eps. **Tz** = exact 16-output latent-space kernel (encoder block).
* Exact one-step decomposition: the true minibatch of step s+1 is replayed (gradient cosine with the one recovered from Adam state = 1.0000, rel. err 0.000 in every ckpt), and Delta theta = H (momentum history) + N (new batch grad: own term for in-batch train points / other batch members) + W (weight decay) exactly; each is pushed through grad L_i, per block and per layer. First-order total vs actual dL_i: corr 0.976 early; the remainder (2nd order) is reported.
* Memorization signature rho = Spearman(log err, LOO dist to class mean in z) as in earlier work, on the 200+200 probes; decoupling = rho_test - rho_train.
* "pull" for a train point = (B-1)/(n-1) * sum_{j!=i} G_ij (expected coupling from the other members of a 128-batch); for a test point B/n * sum_j G_ij. Normalised by the point's own G_ii. **asym** = mean(pull/own | train) / mean(pull/own | test).

## Headline
The decoder carries the memorization (H1, supported), but the mechanism is not "own-gradient share" (H2, only at onset, not specific) and not "kernel becomes sparse/local" (H3, refuted as stated: the function-space eNTK keeps its input-cluster locality and only drifts slightly in rank). What does discriminate overfitting from non-overfitting configurations is a **train/test coupling asymmetry in the loss-gradient kernel**: train points stay strongly coupled to other train points (relative to their own gradient) while test points lose coupling to the train batches. asym is 1.0 at start in all configs, and at epoch 1000-2000: N=250 wd0 40x (+-8, 120x at ep 2000), N=250 wd1e-4 30x (+-3), N=1000 wd0 6.5x (+-1.4), N=1000 wd1e-4 3.8x (+-0.7), full N 1.1x (+-0.1), linear AE 0.9x. Over all 159 (run, epoch>=30) points the log-asym correlates with the decoupling rho_test-rho_train at Spearman +0.79 (MLP only +0.84; linear AE +0.08, no decoupling there). See fig7.

## H1 decoder memorization: SUPPORTED (decoder is where it happens), partly
* Decoder share of the own-gradient norm G_ii (train): grows with overfitting, N=250 wd0 0.845 (ep10) -> 0.93 (ep120) -> 0.94 (ep250), N=1000 0.86 -> 0.91; it DEcreases at full N, 0.80 -> 0.73 (ep1 -> 42; all +-0.01-0.05). The decoder share of one-step improvement is 0.6-0.9 for train and test alike (noisy, sd 0.05-0.25; train in-batch vs test not separable), so the split test/train in step-improvement is not clean; the norm split is.
* Own-gradient decoder self-share (G_d,ii / (G_d,ii + pull)) ~1-1.7 at N=250 ep 30-90 vs encoder 0.0-0.4 (sd large, 0.2-0.7): the encoder is mostly shared, the decoder is self-driven early.
* lr control (fig/`ae_ntk_lrctl_report.py`, 3 seeds, wd0): scaling the decoder lr x0.1 moves the test-minimum epoch 120 -> 469+-38 (N=250) and 120 -> 561+-44 (N=1000); encoder lr x0.1 only 120 -> 195+-14 / 204+-17. The decoder sets the timescale of overfitting onset (gap onset te/tr>1.1: 40.5 -> 107 dec-slow, 69 enc-slow at N=250; 30.7 -> 174 / 53 at N=1000).
* Heckel-Yilmaz rival (layers learning at different timescales): per-layer split of the test dL (fig6, tables) shows the late-epoch improving first-order contribution is concentrated in the decoder output layer (decoder.net.4: -(1.7-2.2)e-3 per step at ep 500-1000, N=250 wd0; encoder layers -0.2 to -0.3e-3 each); no single layer flips sign. Slowing either block shifts t* later but does NOT remove the late rise at N=250 (rise end-min, relative: base 63+-4%, dec-slow 84+-6%, enc-slow 78+-1%; N=1000: 52+-1% / 12+-2% (dec-slow run only reached epoch 2500, i.e. still near its minimum, time-rescaled; not a removal claim) / 38+-0%). So a pure layer-timescale mismatch does not explain the rise, although layer-wise rates do control its timing.

## H2 own-gradient / self-coupling: PARTIAL, not discriminating
* Actual step, in-batch train points, fraction of the new-gradient dL that is own-gradient: N=250 wd0 0.21 (ep10) -> 0.61+-0.18 (ep30) -> 0.55 (ep60-120) -> 0.33 (ep250) -> 0.11 (ep1000); N=1000 0.54 -> 0.37 -> 0.33 -> 0.26. A peak right at gap onset (ep 30-50) then decline: the late decoupling is NOT because own-gradient dominates.
* Linear AE has equally high (even higher) own-gradient share late (ownfrac 1.2-1.6; G self-share 1.3-2.2) yet does not overfit, so self-influence alone is not the memorization signature here; note its absolute improvement per step is ~1e-5 (converged). Per-point G_ii vs latent typicality: Spearman +0.6 (ep 30-120, N=250) turning to -0.2 (ep>=500) when rho_train turns negative: the self-gradient magnitude stops following typicality in the same regime as the error does.
* (iii the self-influence time course is reported either way: fig3, tables.md.)

## H3 kernel localisation: REFUTED as the discriminator (rank/alignment effects exist but are generic)
* Function-space eNTK T: within-input-cluster cosine excess is constant, 0.21-0.24 (N=250), 0.18-0.23 (N=1000), 0.18-0.20 (full), through the whole run; centred participation ratio/n rises slowly 0.02 -> 0.09 (N=250), 0.04 -> 0.14 (N=1000), 0.08 -> 0.17 (full): rank grows, in all configs including no-overfit, so it is not what distinguishes them. Decoder eNTK cosine tracks latent proximity (Spearman 0.6-0.8 test-train) at all times.
* Adam loss-gradient kernel G does become diagonal (cosine-matrix diagonal dominance 0.18 -> 0.69 at N=250 ep250; 0.40 -> 0.76 N=1000; full 0.54 -> 0.66) and loses same-cluster excess (0.21 -> ~0 by ep 60) equally at full N: residuals of neighbours decorrelate in every config. Locality in G is a property of fitting, not of overfitting. (Locality was measured separately from rank, per the literature note.)
* wd barely changes these (G_PRc 0.735 vs 0.733 at ep250; T_PRc 0.068 vs 0.086), i.e. wd does not act through kernel rank/locality.

## What does explain the decoupling and the late rise
Test dL per step decomposes (ep>=120, MLP small N) into: momentum-history term H positive (+1.6e-3 to +4e-3, hurts test; at full N also +1.5-2e-3), new-batch term N negative (-4e-3 to -8e-3, i.e. fresh train gradients DO help test through the shared kernel), and a beyond-first-order remainder +2-7e-3 (zero for the linear AE). Net actual test dL ~ +1e-4 to +8e-4 (noisy, +-1e-3): the late rise is a small residual of three large cancelling terms, so it is not attributable to a single first-order coupling failure (fig7). What does change systematically is the asymmetry above: relative to their own norm, train points keep receiving positive coupling from other train points (pull/own 0.8 -> 9.5 at N=250 ep120 -> 1000), test points lose it (1.2 -> 0.26). Fraction of test points whose train-batch pull is harmful rises 0.04 -> 0.31 (N=250), vs 0.06-0.09 flat at full N.

## Verdict on the four questions
(i) Decoupling: partially explained. Correlational but strong (Spearman 0.84 MLP, n=159 pooled points, absent in the linear AE where there is no decoupling): decoupling appears exactly when train-train coupling decouples from train-test coupling in G, and it is decoder-borne (G_ii decoder share 0.94). Not shown causally (no intervention that changes asym at fixed training). Own-gradient dominance is NOT the cause.
(ii) Late test rise: not explained by any single term; it is a small net of H (hurts), N (helps), 2nd order (hurts). The coupling asymmetry grows with the rise (onset ~ ep 120-250, at the test minimum) but we have not shown it causal.
(iii) wd suppression: modest, partial. wd reduces asym (N=1000: 6.5 -> 3.8; N=250: 40 -> 30 at ep1000, +-3-8) and the decoupling (rho_test-rho_train at ep2000 N=250: 1.18+-0.05 -> 0.95+-0.08) and the test rise (test recon ep1000: 0.507+-0.024 -> 0.474+-0.008 N=250; 0.319 -> 0.273 N=1000). The explicit W term is tiny (-1e-4 per step on test). No rank/locality change. So wd acts through limiting the train-specific coupling build-up, not through the kernel shape.
(iv) Linear AE: no asymmetry (0.5-1.4, noisy), no decoupling (rho_train ~ rho_test ~ 0.3), no 2nd-order remainder, per-step test improvement vanishes smoothly to 1e-5: a bilinear low-capacity model converges to the PCA subspace where train and test errors are tied; its eNTK has no spare directions to memorize residuals (train 0.26 vs test 0.31 at N=250, ep1000).

## Caveats
* n=3 seeds; sd of single-step quantities is large (one-step dL, self-shares with signed neighbour sums can be >1 or negative; use medians in tables.md `G_self_over_pull_med` if needed). G_self_share is unreliable at N=250 (sd up to 1.1); asym, ownfrac_N, G_diagdom, decoder norm share are the stable ones.
* Measurements are at fixed checkpoints at theta_s on one step, not integrated trajectories: the cancellation story for the rise is a one-step audit, not a proof; the second-order remainder is large (Adam lr 1e-3 constant, noise-floor regime).
* G is residual-weighted, so locality/diagonality in G conflates kernel shape with residual decorrelation; T (residual-free) is the clean kernel statement. Probe sets are 200+200 (N=250: 80% of the train set).
* The dec/enc lr control confounds timescale with end-of-training epoch; only the t*/gap-onset shift and the N=250 persistence of the rise are used.
* Storage: runs 2.6 GB, ana 1.8 GB, lrctl 0.26 GB (all /vol/bitbucket). GPU 1 used only for phase 1 (verified freed, 3 MiB).
